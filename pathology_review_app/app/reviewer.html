How this works
  1. Open the .zip you were sent (no need to unzip it). Use the same browser every time — Chrome or Edge is recommended.
  2. Each screen is one cell, marked by a crosshair. Answer the checklist, give a 1–5 score, add notes if you like, and press Enter to save and move on.
  3. Every answer is saved automatically in this browser as you go. You can close the window and come back later: re-open this file and you continue where you stopped (in Chrome/Edge a Continue with … button reopens the same .zip; elsewhere choose it again).
  4. When you are done (or at the end of a session) press Export answers. It downloads a small CSV file named with your initials and the date and time — email that file back.
Your saved answers live in this browser's storage. They are lost only if you clear browsing data, use a private/incognito window, or switch browser or computer — exporting at the end of each session makes that harmless, and Import a previous export restores it.
The cells are a mixture of different kinds, shown in a random order, and a few appear twice. Please judge each one on its own; the order and the mixture are part of the design.

Help

What you are looking at

  • A 150 µm square of a lymph node section from a multiplexed spatial transcriptomics instrument (CosMx), imaged with four immunofluorescence stains. The cell to judge is at the crosshair, in the centre.
  • Composite: DNA violet, CD45 red, Membrane (B2M/CD298) yellow, PanCK blue. Grid shows each stain in greyscale side by side; zoom and pan stay in step across the panels. The single-stain buttons show one channel large.
  • There is no H&E and the defining HRS stains (CD30, CD15, PAX5, MUM1 protein) are not on this slide. Judge morphology only, as far as these stains allow. No CD3 either: a rosette here means CD45-bright small lymphocytes, not proven T cells.
  • Display contrast is set per slide, not per image; use Brightness… if a channel is too dim. Part of the material has a known dim-membrane staining defect, so compare membrane brightness with the cell's immediate neighbours only.

Things worth looking for

  • A large CD45-negative or dim cell ringed by CD45-bright small lymphocytes.
  • A large, lobed or multiple nucleus; dark (DNA-poor) nucleoli on the DNA panel; pale, even chromatin.
  • PanCK should be negative.
  • Some fields are slightly out of focus; blur can mimic pale chromatin. If you cannot judge the cell, answer No to "Image adequate" — that is useful information, not a failure.

Saving

  • Each Save is stored immediately in this browser with the date and time. Changing a saved answer later adds a new version; the export keeps every version and marks the latest.
  • Export answers downloads everything saved so far. Export at the end of every session and send the newest file.
  • In Chrome or Edge, Autosave to file… additionally rewrites a CSV of your choosing after every save.
